The project

What this is, and why I built it

I teach and research in accounting and finance. This began with a personal question: what are the houses in my part of Auckland worth, and how would I know?

The tools I could buy gave me a number, not the reasoning. So I collected the data myself: active listings, council valuations, nearby sales and the price guidance hidden in listing pages. Then I treated it like a research sample. Build the model, test it on data it has not seen, correct for the number of tests, and report what failed.

Some of it worked. The calibrated model is about 7% away from Homes' own estimate on held-out cases, and the 80% interval covered about 80% of them. The text analysis was more mixed: listing language helped when the council valuation was missing, but no topic survived correction. It stays a research signal, not a valuation input.

Method

How a value range is built

01

Recover the price signal

Asking price, guide, deadline or auction date, read from the listing page and dated.

02

Value the tenure

Leasehold, cross-lease and unit title are valued differently before any number is produced.

03

Compare nearby sales

Sold prices weighted by distance and recency, normalised by council capital value where one is available.

04

Calibrate a fallback

A hedonic model for listings with no AVM or usable comparables, with an 80% interval and its error stated.

Limits

What it does not do

  • Not a valuation. These are estimates for research, not a registered valuation, title search or financial advice.
  • Not Auckland-wide. 17 North Shore and Eastern Bays suburbs, active listings only.
  • Not sale prices. Model error is measured against the Homes.co.nz estimate, not settled prices. Where the AVM stops, a comparable-sales or calibrated-fallback route takes over and is labelled.
  • Attributed, not owned. Listing data stays attributed to Trade Me and Homes.co.nz; photographs remain with their owners.
