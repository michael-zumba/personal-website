Auckland Property Scout

Can a buyer see the evidence behind a property estimate — not just the estimate? This project rebuilds an Auckland property valuation from public listing pages, council capital values and sold comparables, then publishes the working: a calibrated range, the comparable sales behind it, and the provenance of every number.

Why I built this

A property question, handled like a research question.

I teach and research in accounting and finance. Most of that work is testing claims against evidence. This project started with a personal question. What are the houses in my part of Auckland worth, and how would I know?

The tools I could buy gave me a number. They did not give me the reasoning. So I collected the data myself: active listings, council valuations, nearby sales, and the price guidance hidden in the listing pages. Then I worked on it the way I would work on a research sample. Build the model. Test it on data it hasn't seen. Correct for the number of tests. Report what failed.

Some of it worked. On held-out cases the calibrated model is about 7% away from Homes' own estimate, and the 80% interval covered about 80% of them. The text analysis was more mixed. Listing language helped when the council valuation was missing, but no single topic survived correction, and in the full model it did not improve things. So it stays a research signal, not a valuation input.

That's the part I think property data usually leaves out. Not a better number, but enough evidence that you can disagree with the number. The map and the research lab are there so you can check the working yourself.

Approach

Why this is different

Automated valuation models are useful, and Auckland already has several. This project is built for a different job: showing the evidence behind an estimate so that a reader can check it. The test is not whether a number sounds plausible. The test is whether the estimate can be interrogated.

How a single-number estimate differs from a source-attributed calibrated range
Question A single-number estimate This project
What you get One number, with no stated interval. A point estimate inside an 80% interval, and the width of that interval is always visible.
What it is based on A model whose inputs are not published. Active listings, council capital values, sold comparables and the recovered price signal — each attributed to its source.
Where the AVM stops Listings without AVM coverage can drop out of the estimate. A comparable-sales or calibrated hedonic route takes over, labelled on the listing rather than hidden.
How to read the number A figure with no reference point. A value-to-CV ratio against council capital value, and the comparable sales behind comparable-sales estimates.
What it admits Uncertainty is not part of the output. Interval, cross-validated calibration error and known limitations are published beside the estimate.
Whether it can be checked Not without access to the vendor’s model. Snapshot, methodology and research lab are public, with the refresh time and pipeline status logged.

This is a comparison of methods, not a ranking of vendors. A single number is the right output for some jobs — a quick screen, a portfolio view, a desktop estimate. The narrower claim here is that for a decision about one property, the evidence should be inspectable.

Method

How an estimate is built

Six steps, each with a named input, a stated output and a guardrail. The guardrail is the part that matters: it says what the method refuses to do.

MODEL — TRAINED ON — SALES 5-FOLD CV MAPE — — INTERVAL
01

Price-signal recovery

Public listing pages are read for the price signals they actually publish: an asking price, a guide, a deadline, an auction date. Nothing is inferred from a headline or a photograph.

Input
Trade Me listing page: asking price, guide, deadline, auction date, description.
Output
A recovered price signal, with its source and the date it was read.
Guardrail
If the page publishes no signal, the field stays empty rather than being guessed.
02

Tenure-aware valuation hierarchy

Freehold, leasehold, cross-lease and unit title are different assets, not labels. The valuation route is chosen for the tenure before any number is produced.

Input
Tenure, title type, land area and the council capital value of the property.
Output
The valuation route that fits the tenure, recorded on the listing.
Guardrail
Tenure is never imputed silently; a leasehold property is not valued as if it were freehold.
03

Independent CV-normalised comps

A comparable-sales model that divides each sale by its council capital value, so properties of different values and ages can be compared on the same footing.

Input
Sold comparables and their council capital values, matched on tenure, type and location.
Output
A CV-ratio estimate that does not depend on any third-party AVM.
Guardrail
The comparable sales are visible, and outliers are excluded explicitly rather than quietly.
04

Calibrated hedonic fallback

When no Homes AVM exists, a hedonic model supplies the estimate. It is validated with 5-fold cross-validation, and the label — “calibrated fallback” — stays attached to the number.

Input
Property characteristics from public records and the listing page, when no AVM is available.
Output
A point estimate with an 80% interval, marked as a fallback.
Guardrail
The 5-fold cross-validated MAPE is —; the interval is always shown and fallbacks are never counted as AVM coverage.
05

Description and sale-pressure signals

Listing text, days on market, sale method and auction deadlines are treated as signals of sale pressure — evidence to read, not motivation to speculate about.

Input
Listing body text, days on market, sale method and auction or deadline dates.
Output
Sale-pressure indicators attached to each listing.
Guardrail
Signals are labelled as signals, never as forecasts, and no claim is made about vendor motivation.
06

Calendar artefacts and limitations

Dates are not neutral. Holiday periods, auction calendars and the age of a listing shape what a snapshot sees, so those patterns are flagged and published with the estimate.

Input
Date fields on every listing, plus holiday and seasonality patterns.
Output
Flagged timing artefacts and a limitations list published alongside the numbers.
Guardrail
Artefacts are disclosed, not corrected away silently; an estimate is not a valuation.

    Provenance

    How the numbers get here

    A scheduled refresh, a deterministic export and a static publish. The same data file feeds this page, the interactive app and the company-site version, so every surface shows the same snapshot.

    1. Daily · 03:15 NZ

      Scheduled refresh

      A launchd job on a Mac mini pulls public listing pages, recovers price signals and recomputes the valuation models.

    2. Checks

      Tests before publish

      The exporter, the snapshot contract and the static app build are tested before any file is copied to a site.

    3. Static export

      One small data pack

      The app and its data are written as static files. This page reads one compact snapshot.json; listing details load in the app on demand.

    4. Publish

      Bounded blast radius

      The publisher replaces only the generated app tree. Authored pages, navigation and styles are not touched by the daily run.

    Limits

    Limitations and fair use

    Every method has edges. These are published here so that a reader can judge how far an estimate should be trusted.

    Known limitations

    • Active listings only. The snapshot observes asking prices and sale methods on public listing pages; it does not observe every settled sale price.
    • Partial AVM coverage. — of standard listings carry a Homes.co.nz AVM; the remainder use a comparable-sales or calibrated-fallback route.
    • Calibration, not truth. The 5-fold cross-validated MAPE is —, and an 80% interval leaves roughly one estimate in five outside its range.
    • Calendar artefacts. Listing dates, auctions and holiday periods create timing patterns that are flagged but not always correctable.
    • Title and tenure complexity. Leasehold, cross-lease and unit-title properties need information a listing page does not always carry.
    • Not a registered valuation. An automated estimate is not a valuation, a builder’s report or a title search, and it cannot replace professional advice.

    Fair use and data

    • Public pages only. The refresh reads listing pages that are already public and keeps request rates low.
    • No listing data redistributed. The site publishes aggregate figures and model outputs; individual listings remain on their original pages.
    • Attribution everywhere. Sources are named on this page and in the app, and corrections are welcome by email.
    • Photos remain with their owners. Listing photographs are hot-linked from the original pages, not copied into this site.
    • No warranty. The figures are provided for research and illustration; no liability is accepted for decisions taken from them.
